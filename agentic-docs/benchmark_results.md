# Benchmark results

This benchmark compared frequency sorting, Anki Miner's i+1 filter, and agentic selection with Luna xhigh and Sol high.

**These scores came from one blinded GPT-5.6 Sol judge, not from human evaluation. Sol also judged cards selected by Sol, so comparisons involving Sol are not independent.**

## Practical result

In this judge run, the four methods scored similarly on accurate anime subtitles. On noisy YouTube ASR transcripts, both agentic methods selected fewer unusable sentences and received higher scores.

The judge classified a card as invalid when it found a malformed or mismatched target, or subtitle text too broken to trust. These labels were not independently verified by a human. The judge-invalid anime cards were not generated by the agents: Anki Miner had already assigned the wrong targets before selection. The agents selected those candidates, while frequency did not reach them and i+1 filtered them out.

### Anime — accurate subtitles

| Method | Mean score / 5 | Invalid rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Frequency | 4.08 | **0%** |
| i+1 | 3.80 | **0%** |
| Luna xhigh | 4.24 | 2%\* |
| Sol high | **4.32** | 4%\* |


### YouTube — ASR subtitles

| Method | Mean score / 5 | Invalid rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Frequency | 3.05 | 38.6% |
| i+1 | 3.21 | 29.8% |
| Luna xhigh | 4.20 | 8% |
| Sol high | **4.86** | **0%** |

## Setup

The inputs were five *Bartender* episodes with accurate subtitles (119.2 minutes, 1,764 cues) and two YouTube videos with noisy ASR subtitles (243.9 minutes, 5,079 cues). Each method requested 50 cards per corpus with the same learner setup and 2,500–25,000 frequency range. The agentic runs used a 600-candidate review pool.

The judge saw the target, reading, sentence, and learner-status flags, but not the method. It scored whether the target fit the sentence and whether the sentence was useful and intact. Judge-invalid rates use the number of cards actually judged; duplicate skips caused some groups to contain fewer than 50 cards.

## Limitations

\* The three invalid anime selections came from two faulty candidates constructed before the agents saw them. UniDic treated the noun `差し入れ` as the verb `差し入れる`, and treated `ご存じです` as a form of `存ずる`, which Anki Miner normalized to `存じる`. The target errors were therefore caused by Anki Miner's upstream morphological analysis, not by either agent.
